# AI writing patterns

Condensed from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup). For each pattern: what to watch for, and the fix. Look for clusters — one tell means little, several together are a confession.

## Content

1. **Inflated significance.** `stands as a testament`, `pivotal moment`, `underscores its importance`, `reflects broader`, `marking a shift`, `evolving landscape`, `indelible mark`. Cut the ceremony; state the fact.
2. **Notability name-dropping.** Lists of outlets or follower counts without context. Keep the one or two citations that carry real context; drop the rest.
3. **Superficial `-ing` tack-ons.** `...highlighting`, `...ensuring`, `...showcasing`, `...fostering`, `...reflecting the community's deep connection`. Delete the participle clause or convert it to a concrete claim.
4. **Promotional language.** `boasts`, `vibrant`, `rich` (figurative), `nestled`, `in the heart of`, `renowned`, `stunning`, `must-visit`, `commitment to`. Replace with neutral description.
5. **Vague attributions.** `Experts argue`, `Industry reports`, `Observers have cited`. Name the source or cut the claim. Never invent a source.
6. **Formulaic "Challenges" sections.** `Despite its X, faces several challenges... Despite these challenges, continues to thrive`. Keep only the concrete facts; cut the frame.

## Language

7. **AI-frequency vocabulary.** `delve`, `tapestry`, `testament`, `pivotal`, `crucial`, `vibrant`, `landscape` (abstract), `interplay`, `intricate`, `showcase`, `underscore`, `foster`, `garner`, `enduring`, `enhance`, `align with`, `key` (adjective). These co-occur; swap for plain words.
8. **Copula avoidance.** `serves as`, `stands as`, `boasts`, `features`, `offers` where `is`/`has` works. Use the copula.
9. **Negative parallelism and tailing negations.** `Not only X but Y`, `It's not just about X, it's Y`, clipped `no guessing` fragments. State the claim directly.
10. **Rule of three.** Forced triads (`innovation, inspiration, and industry insights`). Keep the items that are real; break the rhythm.
11. **Synonym cycling.** `the protagonist... the main character... the central figure`. One name for one thing.
12. **False ranges.** `from X to Y` where X and Y aren't on a scale. List the items plainly.
13. **Subjectless fragments.** `No configuration file needed.` Restore the actor when it clarifies: `You do not need a configuration file.`

## Style

14. **Em and en dashes.** The most reliable tell. Zero in the final text. Replace with period, comma, colon, or parentheses. Also catch ` -- ` and spaced ` — `. (Exception: matching a user-provided writing sample that uses them.)
15. **Mechanical boldface.** Bold on every term or phrase. Unbold unless emphasis is load-bearing.
16. **Inline-header lists.** `- **Label:** sentence restating the label`. Collapse to prose or a plain list.
17. **Title Case headings.** Sentence-case them.
18. **Emoji decoration.** 🚀 on headings and bullets. Remove.
19. **Curly quotes.** Straighten (unless the house style says otherwise).

## Communication

20. **Chat artifacts.** `I hope this helps!`, `Certainly!`, `Would you like...`, `Let me know...`. Delete.
21. **Cutoff disclaimers and gap-filling.** `as of my last update`, `details are not publicly available, suggesting...`, `likely grew up...`. Say what isn't known or cut it; never dress a guess as fact.
22. **Sycophancy.** `Great question!`, `You're absolutely right`. Delete.

## Filler and rhythm

23. **Filler phrases.** `in order to` → `to`, `due to the fact that` → `because`, `at this point in time` → `now`, `has the ability to` → `can`, `it is important to note that` → (cut).
24. **Hedging stacks.** `could potentially possibly`. One hedge maximum, and only if the uncertainty is real.
25. **Generic upbeat conclusions.** `The future looks bright`, `Exciting times lie ahead`. End on the last concrete fact.
26. **Uniform hyphenation.** Keep attributive hyphens (`a high-quality report`); drop predicate ones (`the report is high quality`).
27. **Authority tropes.** `The real question is`, `at its core`, `what really matters`. State the point without the throat-clearing.
28. **Signposting.** `Let's dive in`, `Here's what you need to know`. Do the thing instead of announcing it.
29. **Fragmented headers.** A heading followed by a one-liner restating it. Cut the restatement.
30. **Diff-anchored writing.** Docs narrating the change (`This was added to replace...`) instead of describing the thing as it is. Describe the current state; save change narration for changelogs.
31. **Staccato drama.** Runs of short punchy fragments (`No aesthetic prior. No nostalgia. The old rules were gone.`). One short sentence for emphasis is fine; a run is engineered.
32. **Aphorism formulas.** `X is the Y of Z`, `X becomes a trap`. Replace with the concrete claim.
33. **Fake-candid openers.** Standalone `Honestly?`, `Here's the thing`, theatrical pause-and-reveal. Just say the thing.

## Do not flag (false positives)

Not reliable indicators on their own: polished grammar, formal vocabulary in general (only the specific §7 words), single em dashes in an edited author's own voice, curly quotes alone (editors auto-curl), one short emphatic sentence, one `however`, unsourced claims, quoted or secondhand text (never rewrite watched phrases inside quotations, titles, or examples that discuss the phrase).

## Preserve (signs of a human)

Specific hard-to-fabricate detail, mixed feelings and unresolved tension, era-bound references, defensible first-person editorial choices, varied sentence length, genuine asides and self-corrections. When you see these, edit lightly — over-editing destroys what makes the piece human.
